Merge to AGI Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-09-27

Last updated: 2026-09-27

This policy explains what data the game Merge to AGI (the "game") processes, who processes it, why, and the choices you have. It applies to every version of the game:

  • the Android app from Google Play (package name com.sparetimevc.mergetoagi),
  • the iPhone app from the Apple App Store (bundle ID com.sparetimevc.mergetoagi), and
  • the web version played in a browser on Playgama and the game portals Playgama works with.

The two apps work the same way. The web version is different: it contains none of the partners in section 3, and the portal you play on handles ads, consent and payments (section 4). Where the versions differ, this policy says so.

In short:

  • There is no account, no login, no chat and nothing you type in. Your progress, Qubits, lab and settings are stored on your device (in the web version: in your browser or in the portal's storage). We do not run servers that receive them.
  • In the apps, the game is free because it shows ads. Ads come from Unity (Unity LevelPlay mediation, formerly ironSource, and the Unity Ads network). Unity receives device and usage data to show, measure and protect ads.
  • In the apps, you can buy optional items (Remove ads, a Starter Kit, Qubit packs). Google Play or Apple handle the payment: we never see your card or other payment details. Purchases are checked and remembered through RevenueCat, which works for us and uses a random ID, not your name.
  • In the apps, we use Google Firebase Analytics to see how the game is played and Firebase Crashlytics to fix crashes. Reminders ("Power is full") are local notifications, scheduled on your device only if you allow them.
  • On the web, the game uses only the Playgama SDK: the portal shows its own ads and its own privacy choices, and game events go to Playgama (section 4).
  • In the apps, nothing is asked when you first open the game. After you finish the tutorial, the game asks for your privacy choices where the law requires it, and on iPhone iOS may then ask whether the game can track you. You can change your answers later (see "Your choices").

2. Data that stays on your device

The game saves your level, Power, Funding, Qubits, the merge board and storage, your empire and districts, event and daily progress, tools and skins, sound, music and language settings, your privacy answer, whether you allowed reminders, and the list of store purchases it has already credited (so that a purchase pays out exactly once).

  • In the apps this save is kept in the app's local storage on your Android device or iPhone. It is not sent to us or to anyone else. Deleting the app deletes it from the device.
  • In the web version the save is kept in your browser's local storage, or, on portals that offer it, in the portal's own cloud storage for your portal account (section 4).

If you use your device's own backup (Android backup to your Google account, iCloud on iPhone), the backup may include this save, your privacy answer and, on Android, RevenueCat's anonymous app user ID, so that your progress and purchases come back on a new device. These backups belong to your Google or Apple account; we cannot access them.

3. Data processed by our partners (Android and iPhone apps)

We do not receive your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, precise location or payment details: the game never asks for them. The partners below receive data directly from the software (SDK) they provide inside the apps. None of them is part of the web version.

3.1 Ads: Unity LevelPlay and Unity Ads (Unity Technologies)

When ads are running, the Unity LevelPlay SDK (mediation) and the Unity Ads SDK (the ad network) collect and process:

  • Device identifiers:
    • On Android: the Android advertising ID (also called the Google advertising ID), unless you deleted it in your Android settings (section 5), and the App Set ID, an identifier shared only by apps of the same developer on your device.
    • On iPhone: the Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA), only if you allow tracking in the iOS prompt; otherwise identifiers that are not the IDFA, such as the Identifier for Vendors (IDFV).
    • On both: identifiers Unity generates for the app installation.
  • Device and network data: device model and type, operating system and version, language, time zone, screen size, volume and battery state, network type, carrier, and your IP address, from which an approximate location (country, region or city) is derived.
  • Ad and usage data: which ads were requested and shown, whether you watched, closed or tapped an ad, which reward it was for (for example a Power refill or a doubled level-up), and session information such as when the app was opened.
  • Diagnostics: SDK errors and performance data about ad loading and display.

Unity uses this data to choose and show ads, cap how often you see the same ad, measure and report ad performance and revenue, attribute installs to ad campaigns, and detect and prevent fraud and invalid traffic. With your consent (where it is required) or unless you opted out of the sale or sharing of your personal information, and, for the IDFA, only if you allowed tracking, Unity may also use the data to show you personalized ads based on your activity in other apps and websites.

Unity Technologies and its affiliates act as an independent controller of the data they collect through their SDKs: Unity decides how it uses the data for its own advertising services, as described in Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy (section 14). You can exercise your rights with Unity directly: through the privacy ("i" / data privacy) icon shown in Unity ads, through Unity's "Do Not Sell or Share" page, or by writing to DPO@unity3d.com.

The Unity SDKs use device identifiers and similar technologies (not browser cookies) to recognize the app installation and, with your permission, the device across apps. The opt-outs are described in section 5.

If you bought Remove ads, the game no longer shows ads between moments of play (interstitials); ads you choose to watch for a reward still come from Unity as described here.

We receive from Unity only aggregated reports and, for each ad shown, the network, ad format, placement, country and estimated revenue. We do not receive your advertising ID, IDFA or IP address.

3.2 Analytics: Google Analytics for Firebase (Google)

Only if you allowed it (see section 5), the apps send Google:

  • Game events: sessions (start, end, length), the tutorial steps, level-ups, districts finished, running out of Power, which rewarded ads were offered, started and finished, which ads between moments of play were shown, events joined and finished, projects built, orders skipped, Qubits spent and on what, daily quests, the delivery drone, the shop opened, offers shown and purchases delivered (product, price and currency), and each ad impression with its estimated revenue. Events carry game values such as your level, district and wallet, never anything you type.
  • Events Google collects automatically: first open, session start, engagement time, app and operating system updates, and in-app purchases (product, price and currency).
  • Identifiers: an app instance ID created by Firebase, and on iPhone the IDFV. Advertising storage is set to "denied" in the apps and advertising ID collection is turned off, so Firebase Analytics does not use the Android advertising ID or the IDFA.
  • Device data: device model, Android or iOS version, language, app version; an approximate location (country, region, city) derived from your IP address. Google Analytics does not log or store IP addresses.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the events wait in the app's memory until you answer and are dropped if you decline. In the United States and elsewhere they are sent from the first launch until you opt out (section 5). We use this data to understand how the game is played (for example, where the first sitting ends or when Power runs out), to balance the game and to measure ad and purchase revenue. We do not use Google advertising products and we do not link this data to any account, name or email address. Google processes this data on our behalf.

3.3 Crash reports: Firebase Crashlytics (Google)

Whenever analytics are on (see section 5), when an app crashes or hits an error it sends a crash report: the stack trace, the app and operating system version, device model, free memory and disk space, whether the device is rooted or jailbroken, the app state at the time, the last game events before the crash, and a random installation identifier. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, crash reports wait on the device until you answer; if you decline, they are deleted without being sent. We use them only to find and fix bugs. Google processes this data on our behalf.

3.4 In-app purchases: Google Play, Apple and RevenueCat

Payment. You buy with your Google Play or Apple account, through Google Play's billing system or the App Store. Google or Apple process the payment under their own terms and privacy policies (section 14); we never see or store your card number or other payment details. Google and Apple give us order records (order number, product, price, tax, country and date) that we need for accounting, taxes, refunds and fraud checks.

Checking and remembering purchases: RevenueCat. The apps use the RevenueCat SDK (RevenueCat, Inc., United States) to fetch the prices shown in the shop, confirm each purchase with Google Play or Apple, deliver it, and restore Remove ads and the Starter Kit's skin on a new install. RevenueCat processes:

  • a random, anonymous app user ID that the RevenueCat SDK creates when the game first starts. We do not link it to your name, email address or store account;
  • purchase and transaction data: the product, price and currency, the store's order or transaction number, the purchase token or receipt the store issues, the purchase date, the store country, and refunds;
  • device and app data: device model, operating system and version, app version, SDK version, language and region settings;
  • your IP address, which RevenueCat receives with each request and may use to estimate your country.

RevenueCat acts as our processor (a "service provider" under the CCPA): it processes this data only on our behalf and on our instructions, under a data processing agreement. Purchase data is not used for advertising and is not given to Unity.

3.5 Local notifications

If you allow them (the apps ask once, after the tutorial; on Android 13 and later and on iPhone this is the system's permission prompt), the apps schedule reminders on your device, for example when your Power is full or a generator is ready again. They are created and shown by the device itself: no push service, no notification token and no data leaves the device for them. You can turn them off at any time in the device settings (Android: Settings > Apps > Merge to AGI > Notifications; iPhone: Settings > Notifications > Merge to AGI).

3.6 Apple and Google as app stores

Google provides Google Play, Google Play services and Android; Apple provides the App Store, iOS and the App Tracking Transparency prompt. If you have agreed to share usage and diagnostics data with app developers in your device settings, Google or Apple may give us aggregated, anonymous statistics about installs, crashes and app usage (on Google Play, the Play Console's statistics and Android vitals). Their processing is covered by their own privacy policies (section 14).

4. The web version (Playgama and game portals)

The web version runs inside a web page of Playgama or of a game portal Playgama distributes it to (the "portal"). It contains the Playgama Bridge SDK and none of the SDKs in section 3: no Unity, no Firebase, no RevenueCat, no local notifications.

  • Ads: the portal and Playgama show the ads, through their own ad partners and under their own consent tools, cookie banners and privacy policies. The game only asks the portal for an ad at the moments described in the game (for example a Power refill you chose to watch) and learns whether the ad was completed.
  • Game events: the game sends the same kind of game events as in section 3.2 (sessions, tutorial steps, levels, ads offered and watched, and so on) to Playgama, through the Playgama SDK's analytics. The SDK adds its own information: the platform and game version, a session ID, the Playgama player ID or guest ID, and the device type and operating system. Playgama processes these events under its own terms and privacy policy and lets us see the results; we use them only to understand and balance the game. We cannot link them to your name.
  • Save: in your browser's local storage, or in the portal's storage if the portal offers it (then it is kept with your portal account under the portal's policy).
  • Leaderboards: where the portal supports them, the game submits two scores to the portal's leaderboards (your play time to reach AGI, and your lifetime Reputation). The portal shows them with your portal player name under its own rules.
  • Server time: the game asks Playgama's server for the current time so that timers work across sessions; like any web request this passes your IP address to Playgama.
  • Purchases: the web version sells nothing today. If a portal later offers purchases in the game, the portal handles the payment under its own terms, and this policy will be updated.

Playgama and each portal are responsible for their own processing, as described in their privacy policies (Playgama: section 14). To exercise your rights for data collected by a portal, contact that portal; for anything we can do, contact us (section 10).

5. When data collection starts, and your choices (apps)

  1. First launch and tutorial: no ads, no consent prompt, no tracking prompt. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, analytics and crash reporting stay off until you answer: game events and crash reports from that time wait on the device and are sent only if you agree. In the United States and everywhere else, analytics and crash reporting run from the first launch, and you can turn them off at any time (below).
  2. After the tutorial, the app asks for your privacy choices, depending on where you are:
    • In the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland: "Your privacy", with Accept and Decline, both equally visible. Accept allows personalized ads and analytics; Decline means you still see ads, but not personalized, and no analytics or crash reports are sent.
    • In the United States: "Your privacy choices", with Continue and Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information. Opting out turns off personalized ads, analytics and crash reports from then on, and deletes crash reports not sent yet.
    • Elsewhere: no dialog at this point. Personalized ads and analytics are on, and you can turn them off at any time in the game's settings with Privacy choices (below).
  3. On iPhone, iOS may then ask "Allow Merge to AGI to track your activity across other companies' apps and websites?" This is shown only if you did not decline or opt out in step game or its partners. Android has no such prompt: the Android advertising ID is controlled in the Android settings (below).

  4. Then the ad SDK starts. The app also asks once whether it may send you reminders (section 3.5).

The game plays the same whatever you choose. Nothing is locked behind a privacy answer. Purchases work the same whatever you choose: RevenueCat's processing (section 3.4) is needed to deliver what you buy and does not depend on the privacy dialog.

Change your choice at any time: in the game, tap the gear button (Settings) > Privacy choices. The same dialog opens and your new answer applies from then on (and is passed to Unity).

  • Android: in the Android Settings > Privacy > Ads (on some older versions Settings > Google > Ads), Delete advertising ID removes the advertising ID: apps, including this game's ad SDKs, then receive only zeros.   • iPhone: tracking permission is changed in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking (or Settings > Merge to AGI > Allow Tracking). Deleting the app also resets the IDFV-based analytics identity.

7. How long data is kept

  • On your device (and in your browser): until you delete the app or clear the browser's site data.
  • Firebase Analytics: user-level and event-level data are kept for 14 months, then deleted. Aggregated reports without identifiers may be kept longer.
  • Firebase Crashlytics: crash reports and their identifiers for 90 days.
  • RevenueCat: purchase records are kept while the game sells in-app purchases, so that purchases can be restored and refunds handled, and are deleted when you ask us (section 10) or when we stop using RevenueCat. Your purchases themselves stay with your Google Play or Apple account.
  • Order records from Google Play and Apple: as long as tax and accounting law requires us to keep them.
  • Unity: Unity keeps personal data as long as needed for the purposes it was collected for. Its policy states that app interaction data associated with an installation ID is kept for up to 12 months and transaction records for up to 180 days.
  • Playgama and the portals (web version): as set out in their own privacy policies.

8. International transfers

Unity, Google, RevenueCat and Playgama process data in the United States and other countries where they or their service providers operate.

  • Google (Firebase) is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, its UK extension and the Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework, and uses the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses where the Framework does not apply (Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms).
  • Unity relies on adequacy decisions where they exist and on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses for transfers to countries without one, such as the United States (Unity's privacy policy).
  • RevenueCat stores data with Amazon Web Services in the United States. Transfers are covered by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK International Data Transfer Addendum and their Swiss adaptation (RevenueCat's Data Processing Addendum).
  • Playgama relies on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses where the GDPR applies (Playgama's privacy policy).

9. Children

Merge to AGI is a general audience game. It is not directed at children under 13: it is not in Google Play's Families program or the App Store Kids category, and its target audience on Google Play is 13 and over. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13. The game has no chat, no user-generated content and no account. In-app purchases are made with the store account, where parents can require approval for purchases (Google Play parental controls and Family Link, Apple's Ask to Buy). The apps are registered with Unity as "not directed to children" under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA).

If you are under the age at which you can give consent yourself where you live (between 13 and 16 in the EEA and the UK), ask a parent before accepting personalized ads and analytics, or choose Decline / Do Not Sell or Share, and before buying anything. The game plays the same either way.

If you are a parent and believe your child under 13 has used the game and data was collected, contact us at info@sparetimevc.com: we will ask Google, Unity, RevenueCat and Playgama to delete the data we can identify. Deleting the app removes the local data and stops all collection. Refunds for purchases are requested from Google Play or Apple.
